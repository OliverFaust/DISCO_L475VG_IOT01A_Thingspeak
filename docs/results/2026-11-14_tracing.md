| Configuration | Library | Trace | Result |
|---|---|---|---|

| Debug-Trace | `lib/csp4cmsis-trace` (3.2.0 dev) | ring, 1024 records, overwrite | 0 errors, 0 warnings |

Debug-Trace (`.cproject`): a copy of Debug with `lib/csp4cmsis-trace/csp4cmsis/src` instead of
`lib/csp4cmsis/src` as source folder (so `lib/csp4cmsis` is not built), its `inc` on the C and C++
include paths, the defines `CSP4CMSIS_TRACE`, `CSP4CMSIS_TRACE_BACKEND_RING`, `CSP_TRACE_RECORDS=1024U`,
`CSP_TRACE_POLICY=0U` (C and C++), `CSP4CMSIS_MAX_SYSCALL_INTERRUPT_PRIORITY=5` and
`CSP4CMSIS_DEVICE_HEADER="csp_trace_device.h"` (for the C file of the ring backend too), and the linker
fragment `csp_trace.ld` (`-Wl,-T,../csp_trace.ld`).

Problems met and solved, without changing the library:

- `csp_trace_ring.c` uses `DCB->DEMCR` (CMSIS 6 name); STM32Cube FW_L4 has CMSIS-Core 5.3
  (`CoreDebug->DEMCR`). `Core/Inc/csp_trace_device.h` maps the two names; Debug-Trace uses it as
  device header.
- The linker fragment: a plain `-T` is placed by the GCC driver so that `INSERT AFTER .noinit` finds
  no `.noinit` ("not found for insert"); `-Wl,-T,...` (as in the STM32N6570-DK port) works. Because ld
  reads the fragment before the main script, the region name `RAM2` is not known there (warnings);
  the section is placed at SRAM2's address `0x10000000` with an `ASSERT` on its size instead.
- `docs/formal` (the CSP models) added to `lib/csp4cmsis-trace` from the same commit: without it
  `csp_trace.py` finds no models ("0 checks").

The ring: `csp_trace_ring` at `0x10000000`, 0x4020 bytes (32-byte header + 1024 x 16), section
`.csp_trace` (NOLOAD). `csp_trace_ring_init()` runs from a `constructor(101)` before `main()`;
`csp_trace_ring_tick()` from `vApplicationTickHook()`; `configUSE_TICK_HOOK` and the three
`traceTASK_*` hooks are set in USER CODE Defines of `FreeRTOSConfig.h` under `#if defined(CSP4CMSIS_TRACE)`.

**Regeneration:** after STM32CubeMX `project generate`, `FreeRTOSConfig.h` was unchanged (hooks kept),
`.cproject` kept all three configurations and their settings; CubeMX changed only the internal
"Defaults" string of Debug-Trace (`|| Debug ||` -> `|| Debug-Trace ||`). Debug-Trace builds afterwards,
0 errors, 0 warnings.

## 2. Event rate, ring size and policy

First dump (`2026-11-14_trace_rate/`, ring of 1024 records, overwrite; the upload of 67.0..67.4 s lies at
15.0..15.4 s of its 27 s window, i.e. the dump was taken about 12 s after the upload;
`tools/trace_rate.py`):

```
records: 1024 of 21721 written (capacity 1024, policy overwrite, dropped 0); span 27.000 s; average 38 records/s
records per 0.5 s (peak 687 = 1374 records/s):
  ... 1 per 0.5 s (heartbeat), 59 every 5 s (a sample), 687 in the 0.5 s of the upload
busiest 1 s window: 688 records
```

and at 20 ms resolution around the upload: 0.35 s at up to 90 records per 20 ms (4500/s). By id:
`0x5104`/`0x5105` (thread switched in/out) 359 each -- the Wi-Fi driver's 1 ms polling of the module's
data-ready pin (`osDelay(1)`: Uplink out, idle in, idle out, Uplink in).

| Phase | Records |
|---|---|
| idle, per second (heartbeat) | 1 |
| one sample (every 5 s): Sensors, Console, Uplink take | about 59 |
| idle average | about 13 per second |
| one upload (0.35 s) | about 690 (peak 4500 per second) |
| Wi-Fi start and join at start-up (about 5 s of polling) | about 19 000, inferred: 21 721 records were written in the first 80 s, of which about 1 000 idle and 1 400 in two uploads |

**Choice: 1024 records, policy overwrite.** 1024 is the largest power of two that fits into SRAM2
(2048 records would need 32 KB + 32 bytes). With overwrite the ring always holds the newest 1024
records: one upload (about 690) plus the sample before it (about 59) leaves about 275 records, i.e.
about 20 s of normal work after the upload. A dump within 20 s of an upload line holds the whole
upload cycle with samples on both sides. "Stop when full" would keep the first 1024 records after
reset, which the Wi-Fi join fills within a second.

## 3. Cost

| | Debug | Debug-Trace | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| text | 109 664 | 113 416 | +3 752 B (+3.4 %) |
| data + bss | 48 528 | 64 960 | +16 432 B (ring 16 416 B in SRAM2) |

CPU: in `vTaskSwitchContext()` FreeRTOS calls `traceTASK_SWITCHED_OUT()` and `traceTASK_SWITCHED_IN()`
with only the selection of the next task between them; the time-stamp difference of such a pair is
therefore an upper bound of the cost of one record. Over the 359 pairs of the first dump: minimum 370
cycles, median 411 (80 MHz, -O0): **at most about 4.6 us per record**. At 4500 records/s during an
upload that is at most about 2 % of the CPU; averaged over a 60 s upload cycle (about 1 470 records)
about 0.01 %.

## 4. Capture and decoding

`dump binary memory ... &csp_trace_ring ...`, detach) and `tools/trace_decode.sh` (`ring2log.py`,
`decode.py --no-eventlist -I trace/FreeRTOS_ring.scvd --perfetto`, then `csp_trace.py` and

`decode.py` used its FreeRTOS fallback `--no-eventlist`. ProB is installed (`~/tools/ProB/probcli`).

connect (GDB stopped in `Reset_Handler`); the ring survived (NOLOAD, not yet re-initialised) and the
dump is valid, but the script now uses `-g` (attach): the later dumps did not reset the board (the
console runs on without a new start). The board's flash was read back over SWD and compared with the
Debug-Trace ELF used for decoding: identical (113 576 bytes).

Console of the run: `2026-11-14_tracing_console.txt` (masked). Its first lines are from the previous
firmware; the three starts of the Debug-Trace firmware are the capture's reset, the reset by the first

Three dumps, all with `torn 0`, `orphan continuation records 0`, `dropped 0`, heartbeats 1 s apart:

| Dump | Taken | Files | Window |

| b. upload with a sample inside | right after the upload of 795.37..795.71 s, which a sample (795.515 s) fell into | `2026-11-14_trace_upload/` (with `trace.json`) | 25.5 s, 1024 records |
| c. overwrite | breakpoint on the KeepNewest overwrite in `offer()` (`buffered_channel.h:200`), after a reset | `2026-11-14_trace_overwrite/` | 0.256 s, 1024 records |

**a/b. Ring report clean; library state** (`summary.txt` of b):

```
ring slots: PASS; 13 puts, 13 gets (13 read their put's slot), 0 overwrites (0 evicted the oldest put's slot), 0 dropped (0 without slot)
ALT offers: PASS; 19 waits, 46 enabled guards, 0 unresolved, 48 guards described
ALT deadlines: FAIL 3; 19 timeout waits (19 AltDeadline), 6 ResumeTimeout (at most 0 ticks after the deadline), 10 Resume (10 before the deadline, 0 at it, 0 after)
```

**Timeouts never early.** No `ResumeTimeout` before its deadline in any dump; 6 timeouts in b, each
0 ticks after its deadline (`timeout_ticks_late_max` 0); 10 waits ended earlier by input (`Resume`
before the deadline, as they should). The three "ALT deadlines" errors (in a and b alike) are the first
event of each process in the window -- `sensors` (`ResumeTimeout ... without a deadline wait`),
`console` and `uplink` (`Resume ... after a wait without a deadline`): their `WaitAlt`/`AltDeadline`
were recorded before the window began (checked: none of the three threads has an earlier `WaitAlt` or
`AltDeadline` in the window). They are artefacts of the ring's window, not timing errors.

**KeepNewest on Sensors -> Uplink.**
- In b, the sample of 795.515 s was put while the Uplink was uploading; it stayed buffered and the
  Uplink took it right after the upload: 6 puts, 6 gets on `uplinkSamples`, **no overwrite**. An upload
  (0.35 s) is shorter than a sample period (5 s), so two puts cannot meet before a get.
- An overwrite needs the Uplink busy for more than 5 s: the join at start-up (initialisation 0.9 s +
  join 4.8 s). Dump c stopped on the overwrite at tick 5042 ms; in its window the Uplink and the idle
  thread alternate 253/254 times (the join's polling), `sensors` wakes on its timeout, puts into
  `samples`, and 32 us later records `OverwroteOldest` on `uplinkSamples` (`count=1, capacity=1,
  evicted=0, written=0`): sample 1, unread since 0.03 s, replaced -- exactly while the Uplink was busy.
  `decode.py` reports this overwrite as "ring slots: FAIL 1" because the put it evicted (0.03 s) lies
  before the 0.256 s window; same kind of artefact as above.

**Conformance (ProB):** in a and b, the projections of `samples` and `uplinkSamples` (KeepNewest,
capacity 1) PASS against `buffered_channel_v2` Part 2N (`M_P [T= REC_P`), and the mutation "get-id"
FAILs as it must (`caught: True`); in c, 2 trace checks PASS (one ProB run). Not checked: the ALTs (timeouts of
thousands of ticks; the models cover delays 0..15, rendezvous guards only), `status` (capacity 8, models
up to 4); stable points (none: they need the `ProcessStart` events of start-up, which an overwrite ring
holding an upload no longer has). Coverage of the CSP events: 49.6 % (a), 49.0 % (b).

## 6. Process names in the Perfetto view

The three processes already return `"Sensors"`, `"Console"`, `"Uplink"` from `name()` (used by the
console's stack report and passed by CSP4CMSIS's `Run` to `osThreadNew` as the FreeRTOS task name).
decode.py takes thread names from the `ProcessName` records, which are written once at start-up and
are therefore not in an overwrite ring that holds an upload; it fell back to the symbols of the
control blocks (`MainApp_Task(void*)::sensors`). No firmware change: `tools/trace_dump.sh` now also
reads `pcTaskName` of each control block in the ring's thread records while the core is halted
(`tools/trace_names.py gdb`, written to `ring.names`), and `tools/trace_decode.sh` replaces the symbol
names in `trace.json` (`tools/trace_names.py apply`). Debug-Trace rebuilt: identical to the image on
the board (flash read back, 113 576 bytes). Debug and Release: no source changed, images identical to
section 1 (`c23e71dbd09cbd1f`, `2afbffa9b5dff454`).

Dump d (`2026-11-14_trace_upload_names/`, with `ring.names`, `trace.json` and the masked console):

its trace check is UNKNOWN: `csp_trace.py` writes the window-start get, whose put is not in the window,
as `rd.UNKNOWN`, which ProB rejects ("Unbound Identifier :UNKNOWN"). A limitation of the unreleased
tool with overwrite-ring windows, not a finding on the board; CSP4CMSIS was not changed.
